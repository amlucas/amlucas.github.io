Surrogate Model of a Tank-Treading Red Blood Cell

In a constant shear flow, tank-treading red blood cells (RBCs) move periodically in time. The membrane rolls like a tank tread around a nearly steady shape whose orientation oscillates. Both the shape and the orientation depend on the flow conditions: the shear rate and the viscosity of the suspending medium, . The latent state is split into pairs, each read as a complex amplitude and evolved as a Stuart-Landau oscillator, with the amplitude equation linearized about its limit cycle: The phase therefore turns at a constant rate , while the amplitude relaxes at a rate onto a limit cycle of radius . The flow enters only through these parameters, which a small network predicts from the conditions: a softplus being applied to and so that the relaxation rate and the limit-cycle radius stay positive.

Both equations integrate in closed form, so the state at any time follows directly from the initial state, without time stepping and without anything accumulating over a long rollout.
